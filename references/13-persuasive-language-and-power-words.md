## 1. How to use this file

A power word works because it names something the visitor already wants or fears, and the page backs it up. Pick the visitor's motivation first (Section 4), then the awareness stage (Section 5), then choose language that fits and that the evidence supports. Treat the lists below as a palette, not a checklist: most pages need only a handful of well-chosen words.

All example sentences below are illustrative. Replace the details with the business's real, verified facts.

## 2. Ethical persuasion rules

1. **Evidence first.** A strong word needs a fact beneath it. "Fast" needs a time. "Secure" needs a measure or practice. "Trusted" needs a reason.
2. **No invented scarcity or urgency.** Use deadlines, stock limits or "limited places" only when they are real and stated precisely.
3. **No misleading guarantees.** State exactly what is guaranteed, for how long, and the conditions.
4. **No exaggerated outcomes.** Do not promise results the business cannot control. Prefer "designed to", "helps", "most clients see..." only when true and sourced.
5. **No fear manipulation.** Describing a real risk is fine; inflating it to push a sale is not.
6. **No false social proof.** Never imply popularity, endorsements or numbers that are not documented.
7. **Respect autonomy.** Make the next step and its cost clear. Make declining easy and neutral.
8. **Flag sensitive domains.** Health, finance, legal, safety and children's products need extra care and verification.
9. **Make comparisons fair.** Compare on facts that can be checked; do not disparage competitors.

## 3. Power words by effect

Use words from the effect that matches what the page must communicate.

**Trust and credibility**: verified, documented, transparent, accountable, certified (only if true), independent, proven (only with proof), tested, reviewed, established, licensed (only if true), straightforward, openly, in writing.
*Example:* "Every price is listed on this page, with nothing added at checkout."

**Clarity**: plain, simple, step by step, exactly, specifically, in one place, at a glance, what's included, what happens next, how it works.
*Example:* "Here is exactly what is included, step by step."

**Speed and efficiency**: same day, within 24 hours, in minutes, immediately, instantly (only if literal), quick start, ready to use, no waiting.
*Example:* "Your first draft arrives within three working days."

**Ease and low effort**: easy, straightforward, handled for you, no setup, done-for-you, guided, one click, built in, works with what you already use.
*Example:* "We move your existing data across, so you do not start from zero."

**Confidence and assurance**: dependable, consistent, reliable, steady, backed by, supported, guaranteed (only with explicit terms), clear commitment.
*Example:* "If the work is late, you pay less. Terms are on this page."

**Value**: fair, included, worth, savings (only with a real comparison), lower cost, no hidden fees, one price, flexible, pay for what you use.
*Example:* "One fixed monthly price. Cancel any time."

**Exclusivity and selectivity** (use sparingly and truthfully): invitation, small group, limited places (if real), by application, members only, early access (if real), selected.
*Example:* "We take on four new clients a quarter so each gets senior attention."

**Progress and momentum**: start, build, grow, move forward, next step, milestone, measurable, step up, finish, ship, launch.
*Example:* "Leave the first call with a plan and a date for your first release."

**Relief and removal of pain**: stop, end, simplify, remove, without the, free from, no more, take off your plate, avoid.
*Example:* "No more chasing invoices by hand."

**Security and safety**: protected, private, encrypted (only if true), compliant (only with the specific standard), backed up, controlled access, you own your data.
*Example:* "Your files stay yours. Export or delete them whenever you like."

**Transformation** (highest risk of exaggeration): from X to Y, change, rebuild, reshape, turn around, new way of. Anchor each in a concrete before and after.
*Example:* "From a spreadsheet you dread opening to a weekly report that builds itself."

**Curiosity and invitation**: discover, see how, find out, look inside, take a closer look. Use only where the content delivers what the curiosity promises.

**Belonging and community**: together, join, with people who, shared, member, neighbours, community-led.

## 4. Words by customer motivation

| Motivation | What they want | Language that fits | Support it with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Save time | Less effort, faster results | fewer steps, automatic, ready in, handled | Time comparisons, process detail |
| Save money | Lower or predictable cost | fixed price, no hidden fees, included | Real pricing, plain terms |
| Reduce risk | Avoid mistakes and regret | secure, tested, reversible, trial, clear terms | Guarantees, policies, track record |
| Gain status or recognition | Look and feel capable | professional, polished, respected, standout | Real work, credible endorsements |
| Gain control | Clarity and ownership | choose, customise, own, visibility, transparent | Features, access, reporting |
| Reduce stress | Peace of mind | handled, supported, dependable, calm | Service detail, response times |
| Grow or earn | More customers or revenue | pipeline, reach, convert, retain | Case data (real), method |
| Learn or improve | Skill or understanding | practise, understand, step by step, guided | Curriculum, outcomes that are measurable |
| Belong | Connection, shared values | together, community, welcome | Genuine community evidence |
| Do good | Contribute to a cause | impact, fund, protect, support | Verified figures, reporting |

## 5. Words by stage of awareness

**Unaware**: lead with a relatable situation, not the product. Language: noticing, behind the scenes, quietly, common, many people. Avoid product jargon.

**Problem-aware**: name the problem precisely and show you understand it. Language: struggle, keep running into, wasting, unclear, stuck, costly. Avoid dramatising.

**Solution-aware**: explain approach and difference. Language: approach, method, instead of, different because, built for, designed around.

**Product-aware**: prove it and answer objections. Language: included, compare, how it works, pricing, results (real), what customers say (real), guarantee terms, security, support.

**Ready to buy**: remove friction. Language: start today, get your quote, book, choose a plan, next step, takes about five minutes, cancel any time.

## 6. Words by page and element

- **Hero headline**: outcome or clear category plus audience. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over adjectives.
- **Subheading**: how, for whom, or what is included.
- **Primary CTA**: verb + specific object ("Get a quote", "Start free trial", "Book a call"). Add reassurance nearby ("No card needed").
- **Feature sections**: feature → what it does → what it means for the visitor.
- **Pricing**: plain, complete, comparative. Words: per month, billed, included, extra, cancel, refund.
- **About**: who, why, how you work, what you stand for, evidence of experience. Avoid "passionate" unless shown through detail.
- **Testimonials introductions**: factual lead-in ("What clients said after the first project"). Quotes must be real and approved.
- **FAQ**: answer the real objection in the first sentence.
- **Forms**: "Tell us a little about your project", "We reply within one working day" (if true).
- **Errors**: calm, specific, with a fix ("That email looks incomplete. Check for a missing @.").
- **Success states**: confirm, state the next step and timing.

## 7. Overused words and better alternatives

| Overused | Why it fails | Try instead (when true) |
|---|---|---|
| seamless | Vague; nobody can verify it | "connects to X without manual exports" |
| innovative / cutting-edge | Says nothing specific | Name the new thing and what it does |
| unlock | Cliché; implies hidden barrier | "get", "reach", "access", or the specific result |
| elevate | Abstract | "improve", "raise", with the metric |
| empower | Patronising and vague | "give you the tools to...", "let you..." |
| revolutionise / game-changing | Unprovable | A concrete before and after |
| leverage | Jargon | "use" |
| robust | Empty | Say what it withstands or handles |
| holistic | Empty | List what is covered |
| world-class / best-in-class | Unsupported superlative | Real credentials or evidence |
| solutions | Hides the offering | Name the product or service |
| passionate | Self-description, not proof | Show the work or commitment |
| streamline | Overused | "remove X steps", "cut Y hours" with detail |
| next-level / supercharge | Hype | The measurable improvement |
| synergy / ecosystem | Corporate filler | Describe the actual relationship |

## 8. Turning a power word into a proof-backed sentence

Formula: **Power word + specific detail + proof or mechanism + visitor benefit.**

- Weak: "Fast, reliable delivery."
- Stronger: "Orders placed before 2pm ship the same day, tracked from the warehouse to your door." (requires true cut-off and tracking)
- Weak: "Secure platform you can trust."
- Stronger: "Your data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and only people you invite can see it." (requires verification)
- Weak: "Transform your business."
- Stronger: "Replace the weekly spreadsheet with a dashboard your whole team can open on Monday." (requires the real product behaviour)

If you cannot complete the formula with a true detail, remove the power word.

## 9. When not to use power words

- When the evidence does not exist yet.
- When the brand voice is understated or technical and hype would damage trust.
- In legal, privacy, safety, medical and financial disclosures.
- In error messages and sensitive moments (cancellation, bereavement, complaints).
- When a plain statement is already persuasive.
- When the same word has already appeared on the page or across the site.
- For audiences that distrust marketing (developers, clinicians, procurement teams) unless you lead with specifications and proof.

## 10. Quick quality test

For every power word, answer:

1. What specific, true thing does it describe?
2. Where is the proof on the page or available to the visitor?
3. Would the visitor feel misled if they read the small print?
4. Does it sound like the brand, or like every other brand?
5. Does it help the visitor decide, or just decorate?

If any answer is weak, replace or remove the word.
